# Student Affairs Officer guide

*A plain-language guide for the person doing the job — what you see on screen and what to click. No technical setup here.*

---

## Who this guide is for

You are a **Student Affairs Officer (SAO)** at the school. You are a staff member with broad oversight of the student lifecycle: you decide who gets admitted, you plan the course catalogue and approve lecturers' course plans, you publish students' results, and you settle students' complaints about those results.

In short, the SAO role lets you:

- Review and decide admission applications.
- Build the course catalogue, assign lecturers, and approve or reject course plans.
- Publish students' continuous-assessment and exam results.
- Review and resolve students' result disputes.
- Look up a student's payment standing before exams.
- Manage your own staff profile, password, and display settings.

A few terms you'll meet, defined up front:

- **Matricule** — a student's unique school identity number (for example `stm-2026-0001`). It is generated automatically when you admit someone.
- **Cohort** — the group of students taking a particular course. It is worked out automatically from each student's programme, level, and academic year; you never build a class list by hand.
- **CA (continuous assessment)** — the coursework marks a student earns during the term, separate from the final exam mark.
- **Course plan** — the outline a lecturer prepares for a course. You approve it before the lecturer can run sessions, set assignments, or record marks.

## Getting in

### Your account

You do **not** create your own SAO account. An administrator sets it up for you and sends you an **invite link** (usually by email). The first time, open that link and follow the prompts to set your password. Once your password is set, you sign in normally from then on.

> Keep the invite link private and use it promptly — it is how you claim your account for the first time.

### Signing in

1. Go to the school system's web address. You'll land on the **Log in** screen ("Welcome back — Sign in to your account").
2. In the **Username** field, enter any one of the following:
   - your work email address, or
   - your employee ID (for example `EMP-001`).
   (The small information icon next to the field reminds you it accepts "Email, employee ID, or Matricule".)
3. Enter your **Password**.
4. Tick **Remember me** if this is your own device, then click **Log in**.

If you've forgotten your password, click **Forgot password?** on the login screen and follow the email link to set a new one.

After signing in you arrive at your **SAO Dashboard**.

## What you can do — the dashboard tour

Your **SAO Dashboard** is the first thing you see. It greets you ("Welcome back") and gives you an at-a-glance view of the admissions pipeline.

At the top, a **Review applications** button takes you straight to the applications queue.

Below that, four summary tiles show how many applications sit at each stage:

- **Submitted** — newly arrived, not yet looked at.
- **Under review** — you've started assessing them.
- **Documents requested** — you've asked the applicant for more paperwork.
- **Decided** — applications that have reached a final outcome.

Underneath, a **Decisions** panel breaks the decided total down into Admitted, Rejected, Waitlisted, and Withdrawn.

### Finding your way around

A sidebar on the left links to everything you do:

- **Dashboard** — the overview above.
- **Application review** — the admissions queue.
- **Courses** — the course catalogue, lecturer assignment, plan approval, and publishing results.
- **Disputes** — students' complaints about their results.
- **Standing check** — a quick lookup of whether a student has paid enough to sit exams.

Your name at the bottom of the sidebar opens your account settings (profile, password, appearance).

## Task walkthroughs

### 1. Review the admissions queue

1. Click **Application review** in the sidebar (or **Review applications** on your dashboard). The **Applications** screen opens.
2. You'll see a table of applications, one row each, showing the **Applicant**'s name, the **Programme** (department and degree) they applied to, their **Level**, the current **Status**, and the date they **Submitted**.
3. To narrow the list, use the **Filter by status** box at the top right and tick the statuses you care about (for example, just *Submitted* and *Under review*). The table updates as you choose.
4. Click the column headings **Level**, **Status**, or **Submitted** to sort.
5. Use the pager at the bottom to move through pages, or change how many rows show per page.
6. To work on an application, click **Review** at the end of its row.

If the table says **"No applications match the selected filters."**, widen your status filter — you may have filtered everything out.

---

### 2. Decide a single application

This is the heart of admissions. Opening an application's **Review** page shows you everything you need to make a call.

**What you see on the Review page**

- A **Back to queue** button (top left) to return to the list.
- The applicant's name, the application number, and a status tag.
- An **Applicant** card: name, account email, contact email, phone, date of birth, and previous institute.
- A **Programme** card: department, programme, and level.
- A **Documents** card listing every file the applicant uploaded.
- Decision tools at the bottom (described below).

**Viewing the applicant's documents**

1. In the **Documents** card, find the file you want to check.
2. Click **View** to open it right in your browser — no need to download it. A viewer dialog opens; for PDFs and images it shows the file inline, and you can also open it in a new tab.
3. Prefer to save a copy? Click **Download** instead.

**Moving an application along (Triage)**

Use this while you're still assessing — before you make a final decision. On the left of the decision area is a **Triage** card.

1. In **Status**, choose one of:
   - **Submitted** — leave it in the new pile.
   - **Under review** — mark that you're assessing it.
   - **Documents requested** — you need more paperwork from the applicant.
2. If you choose **Documents requested**, the **Notes** field becomes **required** — type what the applicant must provide. (For other statuses, notes are optional.)
3. Click **Update status**. The page refreshes with the new status.

> Triage statuses are reversible — you can move an application back and forth between these three while you work. They are not final, so the applicant is not emailed at this stage.

**Making the final decision (Decide)**

On the right is a **Decide** card. This is final.

1. In **Decision**, choose:
   - **Admit** — accept the applicant. This turns them into a student (see below).
   - **Reject** — turn the applicant down.
   - **Waitlist** — keep them in reserve.
2. If you choose **Reject** or **Waitlist**, the **Notes** field is **required** — explain the decision. Notes are optional for **Admit**.
3. Click **Submit decision**.

**How to tell it worked**

You're returned to the queue and the application now shows its final outcome. The applicant is emailed the decision automatically (sent to the contact email on their form). When someone is **admitted**, the email includes their new **matricule** — the number they'll sign in with as a student.

After a decision, reopening that application shows a read-only **Decision** card (outcome, who decided it, when, and any notes) instead of the Triage/Decide tools — final decisions cannot be changed.

**Common problems and what to do**

- **"Notes (required)" appears in red and the decision won't submit.** You picked Reject, Waitlist, or Documents requested without typing a note. Add a note and try again.
- **You can't pick "Withdrawn" as a decision.** That's by design — Withdrawn is only used when you merge a returning student into their old record (next task).
- **The application already shows a final outcome and you can't change it.** Decisions are final. If a genuine mistake was made, contact your administrator.

### 3. Handle a returning applicant (prior history)

Sometimes an applicant has been a student here before. When that's the case, the Review page shows a yellow **"This applicant has prior history"** banner listing their previous enrolment(s) and application(s).

You have two ways forward:

**A. Bring back their old enrolment (keep the same matricule)**

Use this when the applicant should resume their previous studies under their original identity number.

1. In the banner, under **"Restore which profile?"**, pick the previous enrolment from the dropdown.
2. Click **Restore prior enrolment**.

This reinstates their old student record (and original matricule) and closes the current application as **Withdrawn** with a note that it was merged. The applicant is notified.

**B. Admit them as a brand-new student (new matricule)**

Use this when they should start fresh with a new identity number.

1. Click **Admit as new student** in the banner. This scrolls you to the **Decide** card and pre-ticks the acknowledgement.
2. Complete the **Decide** card as in Task 2 (choose **Admit**, add notes if you wish).
3. Because there is prior history, you must tick **"I have reviewed the prior history"** before you can submit.
4. Click **Submit decision**.

> Tick the **"I have reviewed the prior history"** box only after you've actually looked at the banner — it's there to make sure returning applicants aren't admitted twice by accident.

---

### 4. Create a course

1. Click **Courses** in the sidebar. The **Courses** catalogue opens.
2. Click **New course** (top right). The **New course** form opens.
3. Fill in the fields:
   - **Programme offering** — pick the department-and-degree this course belongs to.
   - **Level** — choose the year/level. The form shows the allowed range for the chosen offering (for example "Allowed: L1–L3") and won't let you go outside it.
   - **Academic year** — type it in the shown format, for example `2025/2026`.
   - **Code** — the short course code, for example `CS101`.
   - **Title** — the full course name.
   - **Credits** — the credit weight.
   - **Semester** — choose **Semester 1** or **Semester 2**.
   - **Lecturer (optional)** — assign a lecturer now, or leave it **Unassigned** to do it later.
4. Click **Create course**.

**How to tell it worked**

You return to the **Courses** catalogue and the new course appears in the table.

**Common problems**

- **A field turns red with a message under it.** Read the message — usually a required field is empty, the code/level/year combination already exists, or the level is outside the allowed band. Fix it and resubmit.

To **edit** an existing course later, click **Edit** on its row in the catalogue, change the fields, and click **Save changes**.

---

### 5. Assign a lecturer to a course

You can assign a lecturer while creating/editing a course (above), or anytime from the catalogue:

1. On the **Courses** screen, find the course row. The **Lecturer** column shows the current lecturer or **Unassigned**.
2. Click **Assign** on that row. An **Assign lecturer** dialog opens.
3. In the **Lecturer** dropdown, pick the lecturer (you can type to search).
4. Click **Assign**.

The dialog closes and the course row shows the lecturer's name.

> A lecturer needs to be assigned before they can build a course plan for that course.

---

### 6. Review a course plan (approve or reject)

Once a lecturer has prepared a course plan and submitted it for review, it's your job to read it and then approve or reject it. You can tell which plans are waiting because their **Plan** column reads **Submitted**.

1. On the **Courses** screen, find the course and click **Review plan** (rows that aren't awaiting a decision show **View plan** instead). A dialog opens showing the lecturer's plan content. If the lecturer hasn't written anything yet, the dialog tells you so.
2. For a **Submitted** plan, the dialog gives you two actions:

**To approve**

- Click **Approve**. The plan is approved, which unlocks the rest of the course for the lecturer — they can now schedule sessions, mark attendance, set assignments, and record marks.

**To reject (send it back for changes)**

- Click **Reject**, type what needs to change in **Review notes** (required), then click **Reject plan**. The plan goes back to the lecturer with your notes so they can revise and resubmit. When you reopen a previously rejected plan, the dialog also shows your earlier notes.

**Things to know**

- **Approve** and **Reject** appear inside the review dialog only while the plan's status is **Submitted**. A draft the lecturer hasn't submitted yet, or one already approved, opens read-only.
- An **approved** plan is final — it isn't reviewed again. If something's wrong after approval, work with the lecturer and your administrator.

---

### 7. Publish a course's results

Lecturers record CA and exam marks as drafts. Students cannot see anything until you **publish** them. You can publish once the course's plan is **approved**.

1. On the **Courses** screen, find an approved course (its **Plan** reads **Approved**).
2. Click **Publish results** on that row. A **Publish results** confirmation dialog opens.
3. Read the prompt — it confirms which course you're publishing and reminds you that "Students will be able to view their published CA and exam results and raise disputes."
4. Click **Publish results** to confirm (or **Cancel** to back out).

**How to tell it worked**

The dialog closes. Every result that has **both** a CA mark and an exam mark recorded is now published and visible to those students; they can now also raise disputes about them.

**Common problems**

- **Nothing publishes / you see a message that there are no fully-scored results.** Only results that have *both* a CA mark and an exam mark are published. Results still missing one of the two marks are left as drafts. Ask the lecturer to finish entering marks, then publish again. Already-published results stay as they are.

---

### 8. Review and resolve a result dispute

When a student disagrees with a published result, they raise a **dispute**. You review and settle it.

1. Click **Disputes** in the sidebar. The **Result disputes** screen opens.
2. The table shows each dispute: the **Student** (name and matricule), the **Course**, the disputed **Result** (final score, grade, and the CA/exam breakdown), the **Status**, and when it was **Raised**.
3. To focus the list, use the status dropdown at the top right — **All**, **Open**, **Under review**, **Resolved**, or **Rejected**.
4. On a dispute that still needs action, click **Review**. A **Review dispute** dialog opens, showing the course, student, and the **reason** the student gave.
5. In **New status**, choose one:
   - **Under review** — you're looking into it (not final; you can come back to it).
   - **Resolved** — you've settled it in the student's favour or after a correction (final).
   - **Rejected** — the original result stands (final).
6. In **Resolution notes**, record your reasoning. (A note is expected when you finalise; the form will prompt you if anything is required.)
7. Click **Save review**.

**How to tell it worked**

The dialog closes and the dispute's status updates in the table. Disputes you mark **Resolved** or **Rejected** are final — their row no longer shows a **Review** button, and your resolution note is shown instead.

**Things to know**

- A dispute you set to **Under review** can still be reopened and finalised later.
- If the table says **"No result disputes to show."**, there's nothing waiting (or your status filter is hiding them — switch it to **All**).

---

### 9. Check a student's payment standing

Before exams, only students who have paid enough are allowed to sit. To check where a student stands:

1. Click **Standing check** in the sidebar.
2. Look up the student as prompted.

This tells you whether the student is cleared for exams or is short on payment. (Adjusting payments or granting a payment deferral is the accountant's job, not the SAO's — direct the student there if they're short.)

---

### 10. Manage your own settings

Open your settings from your name at the bottom of the sidebar.

**Your name and email** — On the **Profile** tab, update your **Name**, **Email address**, and optional **Phone number**, then click **Save**.

**Your staff profile** — On the staff profile tab you can record your **Scope** (for example "Admissions, Records"). Click **Save**. (Your name and email live on the Profile tab, above.)

**Your password** — On the **Security** tab, enter your **Current password**, then your **New password** twice, and click **Save password**. If two-factor authentication is available to you, you can also enable it here for extra login security.

**Appearance** — On the **Appearance** tab, switch between light and dark display modes.

## Notifications

- **Applicants** are emailed automatically the moment you record a final admission decision (Admit, Reject, Waitlist, or a merge into prior enrolment). Admitted applicants get their new matricule in that email. You don't send these yourself — deciding the application triggers them. Triage steps (Under review, Documents requested) do **not** email anyone.
- **Lecturers** are notified when you approve or reject their course plan via the plan's status changing in their own course list.
- **Confirmation messages** appear on screen as you work — for example after assigning a lecturer or publishing results — so you can tell each action went through.

There is no separate notifications inbox for the SAO role; your dashboard tiles and the on-screen status tags are your live picture of what needs attention.

## FAQ / troubleshooting

**I can't sign in.**
Make sure you're using your work email or employee ID in the **Username** field, and the password you set from your invite link. If your account is brand new and you never set a password, you must open the invite link first. Still stuck? Use **Forgot password?** or contact your administrator.

**I admitted the wrong person / chose the wrong outcome.**
Decisions are final and can't be undone from your screen. Contact your administrator to sort it out.

**The applicant says they uploaded a document but I can't find it.**
Check the **Documents** card on the Review page. If a required document is genuinely missing, set the application's status to **Documents requested** with a note explaining what's needed — the applicant will be prompted to supply it.

**A course plan's review dialog has no Approve/Reject buttons.**
Open the plan with **Review plan** / **View plan**. Those actions appear only while the plan's status is **Submitted**. If it reads **Draft**, the lecturer hasn't submitted it yet; if it reads **Approved** or **Rejected**, it's already been decided and the dialog opens read-only.

**Publishing results did nothing.**
Only results with **both** a CA mark and an exam mark get published. If a lecturer hasn't finished entering marks, those results stay as drafts. Ask them to complete the marks, then publish again.

**A student is complaining they were locked out of the exam hall.**
Exam access depends on payment standing, which is handled by the accountant. Use **Standing check** to see where the student stands, and direct them to the accountant for a payment or a payment deferral.

**I assigned a lecturer but they say they can't set up the course.**
A lecturer needs the course assigned to them **and** their course plan approved before they can run sessions, set assignments, or record marks. Check that you've approved their submitted plan (Task 6).
